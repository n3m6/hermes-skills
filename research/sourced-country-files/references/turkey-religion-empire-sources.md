## Primary texts (verified via curl this session)

- **Treaty of Sèvres (10 Aug 1920)** — Wikisource `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres` is a TOC page; full text at `/All_parts`. Verified clauses: Part IV Protection of Minorities (arts 140–151); Section III Kurdistan (art 62: commission of three members — British, French, Italian — to draft local-autonomy scheme); Section IV Smyrna (arts 65–83); Section VI Armenia (arts 88–89: "Turkey … hereby recognises Armenia as a free and independent State"); Straits Section II (art 37: open to every vessel, no blockade); non-Moslem conversions clause ("no conversions since [1 Nov 1914] are recognised"); clause upholding firmans/hattis/berats privileges of non-Moslem communities. Never ratified.
- **Treaty of Lausanne (24 Jul 1923)** — Wikisource `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne`; Part I at `/Part_I` (arts 1–45, incl. Section III Protection of Minorities). Verified: League Council guarantee ("The British Empire, France, Italy and Japan hereby agree not to withhold their assent to any modification in these Articles which is in due form assented to by a majority of the Council of the League of Nations"); "full protection to the churches, synagogues, cemeteries, and other religious establishments of the above-mentioned minorities"; Imbros/Tenedos exempt from exchange.
- **Population Exchange Convention (30 Jan 1923)** — official Turkish MFA text `https://www.mfa.gov.tr/lausanne-peace-treaty-vi_-convention-concerning-the-exchange-of-greek-and-turkish-populations-signed-at-lausanne_.en.mfa`. Art 1 verbatim: "compulsory exchange of Turkish nationals of the Greek Orthodox religion established in Turkish territory, and of Greek nationals of the Moslem religion established in Greek territory." Art 2 excludes Constantinople Greeks and Western Thrace Muslims.
- **1914 Ottoman jihad proclamation** — `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Ottoman_jihad_proclamation`: five fatwas endorsed by 29 religious authorities; presented 11 Nov 1914 (Rumi 29 Teşrin-i Evvel 1330), published in Takvim-i Vekayi; read out 14 Nov at Fatih Mosque by fetva emini Ali Haydar Efendi (on behalf of Shaykh al-Islam Ürgüplü Mustafa Hayri Efendi — no EN wiki article; cite List of Sheikh-ul-Islams).
- **Lüdke, Tilman. "Jihad, Holy War (Ottoman Empire)", 1914-1918-Online (17 Dec 2018)** — `https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/jihad-holy-war-ottoman-empire/`: fatwa against "all enemies of the Ottoman Empire, except the Central Powers"; Snouck-Hurgronje's "jihad made in Germany"; Nachrichtenstelle für den Orient; Zossen/Wünsdorf POW camps. Author attribution confirmed via fr.wikipedia citation, not byline extraction.
- **Aksakal, Mustafa.** "Holy War Made in Germany? Ottoman Origins of the 1914 Jihad," *War in History* 18:2 (2011) 184–199, doi:10.1177/0968344510393596; and chapter "The Ottoman Proclamation of Jihad" in Zürcher ed., *Jihad and Islam in World War I* (Leiden UP, 2016).

## Key figures (dates/roles verified from Wikipedia raw wikitext)

- **Mehmed VI Vahideddin** (14 Jan 1861 – 16 May 1926 San Remo): sultan 4 Jul 1918 – 1 Nov 1922 (deposed by GNA); caliph to 19 Nov 1922 (succeeded by Abdülmecid II); pro-British sympathies; 17 Nov 1922 boarded HMS Malaya at Tophane with Gen. Harington's help; arrived Malta 9 Dec 1922. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI
- **Malta exiles**: arrests from late Jan 1919 (warrants for 120, incl. CUP notables); exile Mar 1919–Oct 1920; held pending never-held "Malta Tribunals"; released in stages 1921–22 in exchange for 22 British prisoners held by Kemal (incl. Maj. Rawlinson at İnebolu). Exact count ~144–150 [UNVERIFIED — flag in file]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_exiles
- **Occupation of Constantinople**: Allied fleet entered 13 Nov 1918 (Calthorpe's HMS Superb leading; infobox also shows 12 Nov — flag date variants); Allied military administration Dec 1918; British High Commissioners Calthorpe (1918–19), de Robeck (1919–20), Rumbold (1920 – 22 Oct 1923); formal phase 16 Mar 1920 (British raid on Ottoman parliament — Rauf Orbay arrested); evacuation 23 Aug–4 Oct 1923; Turkish troops entered 6 Oct 1923. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Constantinople
- **Chanak crisis** (Sep–Oct 1922): Harington refused to relay Lloyd George's ultimatum; Mudanya armistice 11 Oct 1922; Carlton Club meeting 19 Oct 1922 → Lloyd George's fall. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_crisis
- **Damat Ferid Pasha** (1853 – 6 Oct 1923 Nice): Grand Vizier 4 Mar–2 Oct 1919 & 5 Apr–21 Oct 1920; signed Sèvres; collaborator with occupiers; declared traitor/persona non grata. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ferid_Pasha
- **Ali Kemal** (7 Sep 1869 – 6 Nov 1922): Interior Minister 1919 under Ferid; Peyam-ı Sabah editor (pro-Greek); lynched at İzmit by Nureddin Pasha's paramilitary officers; great-grandfather of Boris Johnson. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Kemal
- **Mustafa Sabri Efendi** (1869 – 1954 Cairo): Shaykh al-Islam in Ferid's cabinets (1919), acting Grand Vizier during Ferid's Paris Conference absence; anti-Kemalist; fled 1922 (150 personae non gratae list) via Romania/Greece; in Egypt wrote polemics accusing Kemalists of colluding "with the British against Islam and the caliphate" — HIS polemic, not established fact; his own pro-British label is inferential (Ferid govt) — flag carefully. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Sabri
- **Rauf Orbay**: signed Mudros 30 Oct 1918; arrested by British 16 Mar 1920; 20 months on Malta; released for Rawlinson at İnebolu; arrived Ankara 15 Nov 1921; PM of Ankara govt 12 Jul 1922–4 Aug 1923. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauf_Orbay
- **Admiral Somerset Gough-Calthorpe**: signed Mudros on HMS Agamemnon for all Allies; British High Commissioner Constantinople 1918–19. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_Gough-Calthorpe
- **Sir Charles Harington** ("Harington of Chanak"): C-in-C Allied occupation army, Constantinople 1920–23; instrumental in averting war. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Harington_(British_Army_officer,_born_1872)
- **Sir Horace Rumbold, 9th Bt** (1869–1941): High Commissioner 1920–23; signed Lausanne for the British Empire. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Horace_Rumbold,_9th_Baronet
- **Sir Louis du Pan Mallet**: ambassador Constantinople 1913–14; Britain's confiscation of two Ottoman dreadnoughts (Reşadiye class) at war's outbreak + Goeben/Breslau context. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_du_Pan_Mallet
- **Abolition of the Caliphate**: 3 Mar 1924 GNA decree; Abdülmecid II deposed as last caliph; Khilafat Movement opposed it; 1926 Cairo and Mecca congress aftermath (cross-ref 03-egypt.md, 04-saudi-arabia.md). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Caliphate
- **Millet system**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_(Ottoman_Empire) ; Britannica https://www.britannica.com/topic/millet-religious-group ("The Ottoman state used religion rather than ethnicity to define each millet").
- **Arab Revolt as counter-jihad**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt — Hussein proclaimed revolt "accusing the CUP of violating tenets of Islam and limiting the power of the sultan-caliph."

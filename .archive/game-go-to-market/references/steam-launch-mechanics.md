# Steam launch mechanics and the fest calendar

Fetched from Valve's own pages and schedule round-ups. **Dates go stale - re-check
Steamworks' Upcoming Events page before quoting any date**; Valve publishes roughly
six months at a time.

## The funnel, in the order it fires

1. **Coming Soon page** - the wishlist collector. Free to publish, no release date
   needed. Valve requires it live **>= 2 weeks before release**; submit for review
   ~7 business days ahead. There is no upper limit, so the cost of an early page is
   the wasted announcement spike, not a rule violation.
   Assets needed before it is public: 4 store capsules, **>=5 screenshots (1920x1080
   min, 16:9, gameplay only)**, a gameplay-first trailer, all 20 tag slots, a short
   description naming the genre and the hook, community hub with discussions on.
2. **Wishlists** - the only pre-launch metric that matters. Wishlist owners get
   emailed at launch and on discount, which is why the base compounds.
3. **Demo** - can be public permanently, independent of Next Fest. For some genres
   the demo converts well enough to justify building it early.
4. **Next Fest** - the biggest free boost available on Steam.
5. **Launch week** - conversion of the accumulated base plus the algorithm's
   response to launch-day velocity.
6. **Themed fests and seasonal sales** - the long tail.

## Next Fest (read before ever recommending it)

- **One participation per game, ever.** Take the LAST edition before the planned
  launch: Next Fest multiplies an existing base, it does not create one.
- Eligibility: unreleased (no previously-launched or re-launched title), not
  participated before, a **published public Coming Soon page**, and a **publicly
  playable demo live before the fest starts**. Releasing during the fest removes you.
- Registration closes **6-8 weeks** before the event. Developer Preview ~5 weeks
  ahead; Press Preview ~11 days ahead (the demo must be live by then). Registration
  can be cancelled before Press Preview.
- Editions run three times a year (February, June, October).
- **Early Access disqualifies you permanently** - EA is a release.

## Wishlist-to-purchase conversion (launch week, P25-P75)

- Strategy / 4X / Tactics: **15-25%** (engaged niche, long evaluation cycle)
- Simulation / Management: 14-22%
- Roguelike / roguelite: 12-22%
- Visual novel / story: 10-18%
- Action / platformer: 8-15%
- Horror / survival: 10-20%

Lifetime 20-40% for an indie launch; the all-genre launch-week median sits near 13%.

What moves the rate:

- **Source beats every other factor.** Organic Steam-discovery wishlists convert
  2-4x better than paid-campaign ones, so an ad-heavy launch underperforms its own
  wishlist count.
- **Bigger bases convert a larger share in launch week**, not a smaller one.
- Price point shifts absolute conversion (under ~$10 the absolute rate is higher).

## Reading Steam's numbers without fooling yourself

- The store page's 30-day figure and the review API's all-time figure differ by
  design: `purchase_type=all` counts key/free activations, `purchase_type=steam`
  counts store purchases. Cite which is which and never quietly take the flattering
  one.
- The store page's "N% of the M user reviews in the last 30 days are positive" line
  is the unambiguous recent figure; the API's `day_range` window is fussy.
- Review counts are a sales *proxy*, never units.

## Fest-ish calendar (as announced, through June 2027)

- Next Fest: Oct 19-26 2026 (registration closed) - **Feb 22 - Mar 1 2027** (register
  by Jan 10) - **Jun 14-21 2027** (register by Apr 25) - October 2027 TBA.
- Seasonal sales: Winter Dec 17 2026 - Jan 4 2027 - Spring Mar 18-25 2027 - Summer
  Jun 24 - Jul 8 2027.
- Strategy-adjacent themed fest on the announced 2027 calendar: **Real-Time Strategy
  Fest, May 10-17 2027**. Valve rotates the themes year to year - do not assume
  "Strategy Fest" still exists.
- Off-Steam and genre-relevant: **TactiCon** (July, ~5 days, Hooded Horse + Firesquid
  co-host a Steam-based convention). Entry is publisher-driven, so it is an argument
  for the publisher route rather than a self-serve channel.
